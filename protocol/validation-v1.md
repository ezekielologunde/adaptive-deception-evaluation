# Fixed validation study after development pilot

Freeze this protocol and code before collection. The 128-world development population remains separate. This is an original empirical benchmark candidate, not a new estimator or a claim of exclusive novelty.

Question: how does retaining observed successful-target identities affect online deception-policy rankings, and how reliably do declared historical-data estimators recover those rankings?

Use the same native simulator actions, three equal-budget stochastic placement policies, memory heuristic, eight encounters and 40 steps per encounter as simulator-development-v1. Stable identities only in this validation: reset and rotated-alias controls already belong to the development population and are not pooled. Two upstream networks: 15-host-network.yaml and 25-host-network.yaml; their three subnet names/order are identical. Log actual blue action targets to verify that mapping.

64 independent worlds per network/memory/policy cell. Two networks, two memory settings and four policies (one logger plus three targets) give 1024 attempted worlds, 8192 encounters and 327680 steps if no failures. Logging seeds 30000..30063 and online target seeds 40000..40063 are disjoint and unused in development. Target comparisons share seeds and are paired. Network sizes share seeds; do not treat sizes as independent replications of a population of networks. Four local CPU processes, no remote or live-target execution.

Outcome: fraction of original server targets without an observed successful native impact during an encounter; mean over eight encounters. This is simulated impact avoidance, not a claim of physical prevention. Native reward is not substituted for this outcome. Audit all three deployment successes, native action traces, memory transitions and source hashes. Retain every failure without silently replacing it.

Primary report: online mean/standard error by world, paired differences between candidate policies with paired standard errors, policy rankings and numerical offline estimates. The offline estimators are prefix per-decision IS, prefix self-normalized IS, and a deliberately history-discarding diagnostic. Neither standard importance sampling nor the fact of adaptive evaluation is new. No p-value fishing or production rates.

Decision support: compute joint bootstrap distributions from 1000 resamples of the 64 independent logging worlds with fixed seed 20261008. Candidate prefix self-normalized estimates use the same resample for all policies. Recommend an offline winner only if its bootstrap frequency of being highest is at least 0.95 and its effective sample size is at least 16 at every encounter. Otherwise abstain. This threshold is an explicit heuristic, not a proven confidence guarantee. Ties count fractionally. Report selection and abstention without claiming that this rule is novel. Online target uncertainty remains visible; do not classify a near-tied online ranking as a proven selection error.

OPEN is excluded from this fixed validation comparison: initial interface controls are insufficient reproduction, and this experiment estimates mean performance over a world from a known initial memory, not OPEN's next-episode forecast under its structural assumptions. Its constant-zero interface control showed numerical drift; do not generalize that observation to an algorithm defect or paper failure. Preserve the interface evidence and disclose the exclusion before collection.

Potential contribution: a public, auditable corpus tying known macro-action propensities to persistent opponent memory, online reference outcomes and identity controls. Prior-art inspection has not established this exact artifact's duplication, but a targeted search cannot prove novelty. CoADAM already evaluates method-specific adaptive attackers; Trident already tests adaptive policies; OPEN and strategic OPE already address changing responses. Full journal suitability requires assessing the actual result and these boundaries.
